# Riverside: Worked Pilot and Follow-Up

This note accompanies the June 30, 2026 follow-up extract.
It describes one worked choice, not the required recommendation for every investigation.

Elaine authorized a pilot for new cardiology and gastroenterology referrals from AP01–AP04 beginning April 1, 2026.
The four referring sites were selected for a manageable rollout, not randomly assigned.
Central intake and specialty staff used an explicit handoff check to identify the next owner and obtain review acknowledgment.
The pilot also added a check for outside care when reconciling an episode.
It did not add specialist appointments, remove clinical-review requirements, or require patients to remain within Riverside.

The intended effect was less waiting between administrative completion and clinical review, with a more reliable account of what happened to the patient.
The check added work and was not delivered consistently to every eligible episode.
Eligibility is identifiable from source site, specialty, and order date; actual delivery is retained in contributor reference evidence rather than assumed from eligibility.

Use the same measure definitions before and after the pilot.
Compare episodes with enough follow-up time, distinguish eligibility from delivery, and examine contemporaneous demand and specialty capacity.
The selected-site before/after contrast is descriptive evidence, not a randomized estimate of an intervention effect.
An increased count of documented outside care can reflect improved knowledge rather than an increase in patients leaving Riverside.
Persistent waiting for appointments can remain after a handoff improves.

Other investigations may justify different bounded pilots.
Assess the evidence, workload consequences, patient-facing outcome, and remaining uncertainty supporting the recommendation.
